# Circumference of a circle

Statement: The circumference of a circle is its perimeter, C = 2πr = πd.
Intuition: If you wrap a string once around a tin, it is a little more than three times as long as the tin's diameter. For every circle the circumference is exactly π times the diameter, and π {pi | 2}, rounded to two decimal places.
Examples: A circle with diameter 10 cm has circumference 10π cm {10*pi | 2} cm, rounded to two decimal places. A circle with radius 1 m has circumference 2π m {2*pi | 2} m, rounded the same way.
Misconception: π times the radius is not the circumference, only half of it. Multiply π by the diameter, or by 2r.
