# Area of a circle

Statement: The area of a circle with radius r is A = πr².
Intuition: Think of a square whose side is as long as the radius. The circle has a little more than three times the area of that square, exactly π times as much.
Examples: A circle with radius 3 cm has area π · 3² cm² = 9π cm² {9*pi | 2} cm², rounded to two decimal places. A circle with radius 1 m has area π m².
Misconception: Twice the radius does not mean twice the area, it means four times the area. A circle with radius 2 cm has 4π cm², a circle with radius 4 cm has 16π cm².
