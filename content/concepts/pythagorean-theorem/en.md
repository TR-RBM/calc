# Pythagorean theorem

Statement: In any right triangle with legs a and b and hypotenuse c, a² + b² = c².
Intuition: Draw a square on each side of a right triangle. The two squares on the legs together have exactly as much area as the square on the hypotenuse.
Examples: If the legs are 3 and 4, then c² = 9 + 16 = 25, so the hypotenuse is c = 5. If both legs are 1, then c = √2. If the hypotenuse is 13 and one leg is 5, the other leg is √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12.
Misconception: a² + b² = c² holds only in a right triangle, and c is always the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle. If you know the hypotenuse 13 and a leg 5, the other leg is √(13² − 5²) = 12, not √(13² + 5²).
