# Chord of a circle

Statement: Two radii and a chord form a triangle whose angle at the centre is the central angle θ. The law of cosines on that triangle gives the chord length s with s² = 2r² − 2r²·cos θ.
Intuition: The larger the central angle, the longer the chord. At θ = 180° the chord passes through the centre, and it is a diameter with s = 2r.
Examples: With r = 5 and θ = 60°, s² = 50 − 50 · ½ = 25, so s = 5, and the triangle made by the two radii and the chord has three equal sides. With r = 1 and θ = 90°, s² = 2 − 0 = 2, so s = √2.
Misconception: The chord does not grow in proportion to the angle. At θ = 60°, s = r, but at twice the angle, θ = 120°, s = r√3 and not 2r.
