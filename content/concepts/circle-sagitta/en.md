# Sagitta of a circle

Statement: The sagitta h of a chord s is the distance from the midpoint of the chord to the arc. The Pythagorean theorem on the half chord gives (r − h)² + (s/2)² = r², so r = h/2 + s²/(8h).
Intuition: The sagitta says how far an arc bulges above its chord, like the arrow in a drawn bow. If you measure the chord and the sagitta on a piece of a circle, you can compute the radius of the whole circle, even when you cannot see its centre.
Examples: With chord s = 8 and sagitta h = 2, r = 2/2 + 64/16 = 1 + 4 = 5. With s = 10 and h = 5, r = 5/2 + 100/40 = 5, and the chord is a diameter.
Misconception: The sagitta is measured at the middle of the chord, at a right angle to the chord, up to the arc. It is measured neither at the end of the chord nor from the centre of the circle.
