# Law of cosines

Statement: In any triangle with sides a, b, c and the angle γ opposite c, c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cos γ.
Intuition: The law of cosines is the Pythagorean theorem with one extra term. When γ = 90°, cos γ = 0 and what remains is c² = a² + b². When γ is larger than 90°, c gets longer, and when γ is smaller, c gets shorter.
Examples: With a = 3, b = 5 and γ = 60°, c² = 9 + 25 − 2 · 3 · 5 · ½ = 19, so c = √19. With a = 3, b = 5 and γ = 120°, cos γ = −½, so c² = 9 + 25 + 15 = 49 and c = 7.
Misconception: γ must be the angle opposite the side c, which is the angle between a and b. If you put in another angle of the triangle, you get a wrong c.
