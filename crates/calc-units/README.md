# calc-units

## What it does

Holds units and dimensions.

`Dimension` is a dimension product: eight integer exponents over length, mass, time, electric current, thermodynamic temperature, amount of substance, luminous intensity and information, in that order. The first seven are the SI base quantities; information is the eighth so that an amount of data is a quantity of its own and never adds to a bare number. Products add exponents and powers multiply them. Exponents are `i8`, and a result outside that range is `DimensionError::ExponentOutOfRange`.

`ScaleFactor` is an exact positive rational times an integer power of pi. It is stored reduced. It multiplies, divides, inverts and raises to an `i8` power exactly. A pi exponent outside `i32` is `ScaleFactorError::PiExponentOutOfRange`.

`UnitTable` interns units. A `UnitId` stands for one unit product: a list of named units with nonzero `i8` exponents, sorted by symbol, with its dimension and its scale factor relative to the coherent SI unit of that dimension. Two unit products are the same unit exactly when their ids are equal. Equal symbols with exponents that cancel, such as `m/m`, give the dimensionless unit. Different symbols never cancel, so `m/km` is a dimensionless unit with scale factor 1/1000.

A new table holds the seven SI base units `m`, `kg`, `s`, `A`, `K`, `mol`, `cd`, the twenty-one coherent derived units with their own names, the gram `g` as the unit that decimal prefixes attach to for mass, and `degC` for a temperature difference, equal to the kelvin with scale factor 1 but kept as its own unit. `lookup` resolves a name. A full unit name wins over a prefix reading, so `cd` is the candela. Otherwise a name is one of the twenty-four decimal prefixes followed by a unit that accepts a prefix, and the prefixed unit is registered on first use. `kg`, `degC` and already prefixed units take no prefix. The micro sign U+00B5 and the Greek mu U+03BC are both the prefix micro. The Greek omega U+03A9 and the ohm sign U+2126 are both the ohm.

`multiply`, `divide` and `power` build unit products. `coherent_unit` returns the product of base units for a dimension. `named_coherent_unit` returns the one SI base or derived unit with its own name, scale factor 1 and that dimension, such as `N` for `kg*m*s^-2`, and falls back to the product of base units when there is none or more than one, as for `Hz` and `Bq`. `conversion_factor` returns the exact factor from one unit to another of the same dimension and `ConversionError::DimensionMismatch` otherwise. Each table has at most `u32::MAX` units and named units. Adding past that is `TableFull`. An id from another table is `UnknownUnitId`.

The table also holds `deg`, the degree of plane angle, with the exact scale factor π/180 to the radian and dimension one like the radian. It is the one unit so far whose scale factor carries a power of π. It takes no prefix. An angle carries its unit in the expression, and no angle mode exists: the sine of an angle in degrees is converted to radians by this scale factor, so `sin(30 deg)` is exactly 1/2.

The units outside the SI have these exact scale factors to the coherent unit:
- `min` 60 s, `h` 3600 s, `d` 86400 s, `L` or `l` 1/1000 m³ and `t` 1000 kg. The source is the BIPM SI Brochure, 9th edition (2019), Table 8, which is also the source of `deg`.
- `in` 127/5000 m, `ft` 381/1250 m, `yd` 1143/1250 m, `mi` 201168/125 m and `lb` 45359237/100000000 kg. The source is the international yard and pound agreement of 1959, as published in NIST Special Publication 811 (2008), Appendix B.
- `degF` 5/9 K, a temperature difference like `degC`.

Only the litre takes a prefix, and `ml` and `mL` are the same unit. No new spelling collides with a prefix reading: `min` is the minute, never a prefixed `in`, because the inch takes no prefix.

`display_symbol` returns the output symbol of a named unit. It equals the symbol except `°C` for `degC`, `°F` for `degF`, `°` for `deg`, `L` for the spelling `l`, and the Greek omega for the ohm sign. A prefixed unit shows the prefix symbol before the unit's display symbol. Display symbols are never parsed.

`TemperatureScale` is the interval scale table, with `Kelvin`, `Celsius` and `Fahrenheit`. A reading `t` maps to the thermodynamic temperature `T = (t + offset) · factor` in kelvin: `offset` is 0, 5463/20 and 45967/100, and `factor` is 1, 1 and 5/9. `offset_ratio` and `factor_ratio` give the same coefficients as a `NonNegativeRatio` and a `PositiveRatio` with `NonZeroU64` parts, so a caller can build exact maps that cannot divide by zero. `to_kelvin` and `from_kelvin` are exact on exact numbers, and a machine number is `TemperatureScaleError::MachineReading`. `factor` is also the factor for scaling a difference or an uncertainty, without the offset. `name` and `from_name` use the scale names `kelvin`, `celsius` and `fahrenheit` of unit declarations, and `display_symbol` gives `K`, `°C` and `°F`. A scale is not a unit, so `lookup` never returns one.

`prefix_family` gives the unprefixed named unit that a prefixed one belongs to, with the kilogram counted in the gram family, so the readable pick of a display unit finds a written unit's prefixed relatives.

Conversion for display. `convert_for_display(table, value, coherent, target, significant_digits)` takes a value in its coherent unit and a `DisplayTarget`, a unit of the same dimension or a temperature scale for a value in kelvin. An exact value times a rational factor, or on a scale through the exact affine map, is `DisplayedNumber::Exact`. A finite machine value is taken as the exact rational its bits denote, converted exactly, and rounded once to nearest with ties to even at the significant digits the display asks for, or at 17 for `f64` and 9 for `f32` where the value is printed in full, as `Rounded`, so no later rounding works on an already rounded number. A factor with a power of pi, such as `deg`, is correctly rounded from the exact product: `correctly_rounded_to_significant_digits` of `calc-numbers` refines ball enclosures until both ends round to the same decimal. An exact value whose conversion would carry pi is `ExactValueWouldBeRounded`, because an exact value is never approximated. NaN, infinities and a negative machine zero under a ratio unit come back `Unscaled`. `scale_spread_for_display` scales an uncertainty or a bound by the same factor without the offset of an affine map: exact times rational stays exact, and every other product is rounded upward to the smallest `f64` at least the exact product, from an enclosure's upper end when the factor carries pi, so a shown spread is never smaller than the stored one. `convert_pi_multiple_for_display` converts an exact `c·pi^k` in its coherent unit by a factor `r·pi^m`: when `k + m` is 0, as for `deg` from radians with `k = 1` or `deg^2` with `k = 2`, pi cancels and gives the exact rational `Rational(c·r)`, otherwise `PiPower { c·r, k + m }`, and any other target, a temperature scale included, is `ExactValueWouldBeRounded`.

The table also holds the electronvolt `eV` and the bar, both taking decimal prefixes, and the standard atmosphere `atm`, which takes none. There is no plain `cal`: the thermochemical calorie `cal_th` (4.184 J) and the International Table calorie `cal_IT` (4.1868 J) both take decimal prefixes, and a bare `cal`, `kcal` or `Cal` is refused with both readings named. `ambiguous_readings` does the same for `yr`, `gal`, `oz` and `hp`, whose unambiguous units (`a`, `gal_US`, `gal_imp`, `oz_av`, `oz_t`, `floz_US`, `floz_imp`, `hp_I`, `hp_M`, `hp_E`) are in the table. The units `Wh`, `ha`, `au`, `pc`, `ly`, `nmi`, `kn`, `psi` and `mmHg` are here too, and so are `arcmin`, `arcsec`, `mil_NATO`, `IPHY` and `thou`; `MOA` and `mil` are refused with their readings. The table also holds the torr, `Torr` (also `torr`), 101 325/760 Pa with decimal prefixes, which is not the `mmHg`. A definition's scale factor is a rational, a power of ten and a power of π, and the power of ten is what lets the electronvolt keep its exact factor, 1 602 176 634 · 10⁻²⁸ J, whose denominator does not fit a `u64`.

The week is one unit with two spellings, `wk` and `week`, at 604 800 s, seven days of 86 400 s, and it takes no decimal prefix. It shows as `wk` whichever spelling was written, and it is a duration and not a calendar period: nothing here does calendar arithmetic, so a week is seven days exactly, as a day is 86 400 s exactly.

Information has the base unit `bit` and the byte `B` of 8 bit, as IEC 80000-13 and NIST's page on binary prefixes have them. Both take the decimal prefixes from `k` upward, powers of 1000, and the binary prefixes `Ki`, `Mi`, `Gi`, `Ti`, `Pi` and `Ei`, powers of 1024, which apply to them alone. No decimal prefix below `k` attaches to them, so `mB` is not a unit rather than a millibyte written for a megabyte. `ambiguous_readings` names the spellings in use for more than one unit and the two that mean one each: `KB` is `kB` or `KiB`, `Kbit` is `kbit` or `Kibit`, and `b` behind a prefix, as in `Mb`, is `Mbit` or `MB`. `lookup` refuses them as `UnitLookupError::Ambiguous` rather than choosing one. `has_binary_prefix` says whether a unit holds a factor with a binary prefix, which the form of an answer needs.

## How to test

`cargo test -p calc-units`

Derived units are checked against their defining relations, such as `F = C/V` and `Gy = J/kg`, not against the table they come from. Units outside the SI are checked against their defining relations, such as `ft = 12 in` and `L = dm³`, and temperature scales against fixed points, such as 32 °F and 0 °C. Every prefix on every unit that accepts one is checked against the SI prefix table written out in the test.
