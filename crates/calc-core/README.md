# calc-core

## What it does

The math core: mathematical algorithms on expressions, such as simplification, differentiation, dimension checks, exact evaluation, and numerical methods that submit plans to the execution layer.

So far it holds `ComputedResult`, the part of a result record that the core supplies. It carries the kind, the value, the unit, the rounding error, the measurement uncertainty, the method with its parameters and diagnostics, the corpus references, the seed and the recognized concepts. The members follow the session record.

`ComputedResult::new` checks that the kind matches the value: `ExactRational` needs exact numbers, `MachineFloat` needs `F32` or `F64` numbers, and an expression value is `Algebraic` or `Symbolic` and exact, with rounding error `None`. An exact result starts with rounding error `None`, a machine result with `Unknown` until `with_rounding_bound` sets a bound. A bound must be a non-negative exact or `F64` value. An array value must have as many elements as its shape describes. `Uncertainty::new` stores the standard uncertainty and a coverage factor of at least 1. Corpus references are kept sorted and without duplicates.

`ResultValue::Expression` holds the record's `expression` value as printed text. `calc-core` never builds that text, because printing is in `calc-syntax`; `calc-app` fills it, for example as the reading of an error. `ComputedResult::new` does not yet accept an expression value, so `Algebraic` and `Symbolic` results are still rejected. `Recognition` is always empty until recognition exists.

`evaluate_exact` evaluates an expression of the pool exactly and returns an `ExactEvaluation`: the kind, the id of the evaluated expression, and the number when the kind is `ExactRational`. Exact evaluation ignores the written shape.

- Rationals are computed exactly. `0.1 + 0.2` is `3/10`.
- Square roots are kept exactly as sums of rational multiples of square roots of square-free integers, for example `sqrt(8)` is `2*sqrt(2)` and `1/(1 + sqrt(2))` is `-1 + sqrt(2)`. Such a sum is zero only when it is written as zero, so a value that reduces to a rational is always reported as `ExactRational`. Signs are decided exactly by squaring out one prime at a time, so `Abs`, `Min`, `Max`, `CopySign`, `Floor`, `Ceil`, `Trunc` and `RoundTiesEven` are exact on these values. A radicand whose square-free part cannot be certified by trial division up to 2^16 stays an unevaluated `Sqrt` of kind `Algebraic`.
- `Pow` with an integer exponent uses the monoid and group power, so `0^0` is 1 and `0^-1` is a division by zero. A non-integer exponent needs a positive base, so `0^(1/2)` and `(-8)^(1/3)` are outside the domain. A rational power of a rational is rational when the root is exact, otherwise it stays an unevaluated `Pow` of kind `Algebraic`.
- Rational multiples of `pi` are tracked exactly: `pi - pi` is 0 and `pi/6 + pi/3` is `1/2*pi`. `sin`, `cos` and `tan` of a multiple of `pi` with denominator 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 give exact values; other rational multiples stay unevaluated with kind `Algebraic`. `asin`, `acos`, `atan` and `atan2` of those exact values give multiples of `pi`. `exp(0)` is 1, `exp(1)` is `e`, `ln(1)` is 0 and `ln(e)` is 1.
- Everything else, such as free variables, function applications, `exp(2)` or `sin(1)`, is kept as an expression with the evaluated arguments and has kind `Symbolic`. `Symbolic` means that no exact number was found, not that the value is transcendental.
- Exact values have a fixed size limit, `EXACT_RESULT_BIT_LIMIT` = 2^18 = 262144 bits.
  - The limit is a constant, never taken from the machine's memory, so a replay gives the same answer everywhere.
  - A value whose numerator, denominator or radicand needs more bits is `ResultTooLarge`, with the limit and the estimated decimal digit count.
  - Every evaluated node is checked.
  - `pow` and `!` first bound the result size from below, so `10^4000000000` or `1000000!` is refused before any digit is built. The lower bounds are `n*(b-1)+1` bits for the n-th power of a rational whose larger part has b bits, and the sum of `floor(log2 k)` plus 1 for `n!`. A power of a square root sum has no lower bound. It is computed by square-and-multiply, and the size is checked after every product, so no step exceeds twice the limit. It is refused when a step exceeds the limit.
  - When only the upper bound exceeds the limit, the value is computed, which takes at most twice the limit, and its real size decides.
  - For a refused power of a rational, the digit estimate is `n*log2(base)*0.30103 + 1`, with `log2` taken exactly to 32 fractional bits in integer arithmetic from the top 63 bits of the base. So `2^262144` reports 78914 digits, and `10^4000000000` reports 4000000058 against the true 4000000001. Otherwise it is `bits*0.30103 + 1`.
  - `IntegerArgumentOutOfRange` carries the argument and its limit, u32::MAX, for an exponent, a root degree or a factorial argument. `10^100^10` names the exponent 10^20.
  - The limit was chosen by measuring the three costs of the largest allowed result on the development host (AMD EPYC-Milan, 4 cores, release build): computing `2^262143` takes 36 ms, converting it to its 78913 decimal digits in `calc-app` takes 325 ms, writing them takes under 1 ms, and `calc "2^262143"` runs in 0.26 s. Decimal conversion is quadratic in the digits, so 2^19 bits took 1.39 s to convert and 2^20 bits took 5.1 s. `20000!` with 77,338 digits runs in 0.36 s.
- Machine numbers are an error, except as the argument of `ToExact`, which gives the rational the binary value denotes. Comparisons, logic, `Select`, `Complex`, `ToF32`, `ToF64`, uncertainty, unit conversion, binders, quantities and arrays are not evaluated and return an error.
- Expressions are evaluated without recursion, so deep expressions do not exhaust the stack. Shared subexpressions are evaluated once.

Exact evaluation carries a rational times an integer power of pi, `c·pi^k`, as its own value form: products and quotients add and subtract the powers, sums and differences of equal powers add the coefficients, an integer power of such a value multiplies the exponent, and a quotient of equal powers is rational. Sums of different powers, roots and elementary functions of such a value stay symbolic, and a negative one is outside the domain of `ln`, `sqrt` and a fractional power. `ExactEvaluation::pi_power` gives `(c, k)` for such a result, with `k` never 0, so a caller can keep it exact without printing or parsing it.

`evaluate_f64` evaluates an expression in `F64` machine arithmetic. It lowers the expression to a plan with `calc_exec::lower`, selects a backend from the ones the caller passes with the caller's preference, runs the plan, and returns a `MachineEvaluation`: a `ComputedResult` of kind `MachineFloat` with an `F64` value, or a complex value of two `F64` parts, plus the selected backend, the skipped ones, and whether the plan contains an approximate operation, for the record. A root `Sum` or `Product` with constant integer bounds runs as a map over the index channel and a reduction. The method is `plan_evaluation` with the parameter `domain` set to `f64`, and `reduction_shape` for a reduction. `evaluate_f32` does the same in the `F32` plan domain: exact leaves are rounded to `f32` at their constants, approximate operations run the `f32` references of `calc-numbers`, and the value is an `F32` number with `domain` `f32`. Its rounding bound comes from the same `f64` enclosure of the exact value, measured to the `f32` value widened to `f64`, so it covers the rounding of the constants, of every gate and the one-ulp error of the references. A machine `F64` operand in an `F32` evaluation is a lowering error. `calc-core` does not depend on a backend crate, so the backends come from `calc-app`. The tests use `calc-exec-cpu`.

The rounding error bound is a rigorous enclosure of the exact value of the same expression, computed by a second evaluation of the expression in interval arithmetic from `calc-numbers` (`Interval`). Exact numbers enter as the two machine numbers around them, `pi` and `e` as the machine numbers around the rounded constants, and every operation widens its result outward. The exact value is the real value of the expression with conversion operators taken as the identity, so rounding at the leaves counts as rounding error. The bound is the larger distance from the computed value to the two ends of the enclosure, rounded upward. Where no enclosure exists, the rounding error is `Unknown`: a division by an interval containing zero, a logarithm or square root at or below zero, a tangent pole, the branch cut of `atan2`, a power of a base interval that is not positive with an exponent that is not an integer, a negative integer power of a base interval containing zero, `CopySign` with a sign interval containing zero, a complex operation other than negation, addition, subtraction and multiplication, and any non-finite result. An undecided comparison in `Select` gives the hull of both branches. An integer power of a negative base interval is the power of its mirror image, negated for an odd exponent, so the enclosure of `x^2` is the same at `-x` as at `x`, and an even power of an interval across zero starts at zero.

`enclose_decimal(pool, expression, n, is_cancelled)` gives the decimal enclosure of an expression to `n` significant digits, with `n` up to 5000. It checks `is_cancelled` between precision steps and returns the `Cancelled` error of `calc-numbers` when it answers true, because `calc-app` runs it off the session thread. It evaluates exactly first: an exact rational is rounded outward by `enclose_exact`, and an algebraic or symbolic result is enclosed from its simplified expression. An expression that exact evaluation rejects for a machine literal or an unsupported node is enclosed from its coherent form, where a machine literal stands for its exact binary value. Enclosing walks the expression with `RealBall` at each precision of the Ziv loop of `calc-numbers`. The operators with a proven enclosure are `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, negation, `abs`, `sqrt`, powers (an integer literal exponent by repeated squaring, any other through `exp(y·ln x)`), `exp`, `ln`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan` and the constants `pi` and `e`. `to_f64(x)` and `to_f32(x)` stand for their machine result: `x` is computed in that format with its exact leaves and `pi` and `e` rounded once and only correctly rounded operations (`+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, negation, `abs`, `sqrt`, `mul_add`, rounding, `copysign`, `min`, `max`), as its lowered plan computes on every backend, and the result's exact bits are enclosed. An approximate operation, a conversion to the other format or a non-finite result inside makes it `NotApplicable`. A power whose exponent evaluates exactly to an integer, such as `x^(1+2)`, uses repeated squaring, so a negative base keeps its value. Every other node, including uncertainties, free variables, complex values and binders, is `NotApplicable`, and so is an expression whose exact evaluation fails with a division by zero, a domain error, an argument out of range or a result too large, or for which no precision within the budget gives an enclosure. A value whose numbers outgrow the 17032-bit enclosure budget before any interval is proven is the `OverBudget` error of `calc-numbers`, and one that outgrows it later keeps its proven interval with `reached` false. A digit count outside 1 to 5000 is the `Enclosure` error of `calc-numbers`.

`scaled_expression_at_least_one` and `round_scaled_expression` enclose an expression multiplied by a rational and a power of pi, the conversion factor of a display unit: the first says whether the magnitude reaches 1, for the choice of a readable unit, the second gives the correctly rounded value at a number of significant digits, for a readable result. Both refine within the enclosure budget and answer None where the enclosure does not reach the question.

`enclose_sample` gives the same enclosure for one sample of a picture. Each `SampleInput` names a symbol and its exact position, real or complex, and the position enters as the two machine numbers around it. The expression is not converted to coherent units, because sampling lowers the expression it is given. `SampleEnclosure::rounding_bound` takes the value the plan returned, an `f32` value widened to `f64` for an `F32` plan, and gives the larger distance to the ends of the enclosure rounded upward, or NaN when no enclosure exists, which a picture reads as unknown. `complex_rounding_bounds` does the same per part. For `(1 - cos(x)) / x^2` the bound is about `2.2e-16 / x^2`, so over a value interval of width 0.2 with 1000 divisions a sample is resolved at `x = 1e-5` and unresolved at `x = 1e-7`.

The module `graph` holds directed graphs with layering and transitive reduction. `calc-app` uses it for the Board placement, and `calc-viz` for graph scenes. It has its own README in `src/graph/`.

`to_coherent_units` prepares an expression that contains `Quantity` nodes for evaluation, and both `evaluate_exact` and `evaluate_f64` call it first. It checks dimensional homogeneity: sums, differences, minimum, maximum, comparisons, `atan2` and the branches of `select` need one dimension; products and quotients combine dimensions; integer and rational powers of a quantity need a constant exact exponent and whole resulting exponents; square roots need even exponents; elementary functions, rounding, factorial and user functions need dimensionless arguments. A violation is a typed `QuantityError`. Each quantity becomes its value times the exact scale factor of its unit, so the expression holds numbers in coherent SI units. The unit of the result is the one named SI unit of that dimension when exactly one exists, such as `N`, and otherwise the product of base units, such as `m*s^-1`. A conversion `x -> u` at the root divides by the scale factor of `u` and gives the unit `u`; inside an expression it keeps the coherent value. `ExactEvaluation::unit` and the `unit` of the machine `ComputedResult` carry the unit, and the rounding bound shares it.

The temperature conversions are affine maps `a*x + b`, whose exact coefficients `TemperatureConversion::affine_map` takes from `TemperatureScale` in `calc-units`. For `from_celsius` and `from_fahrenheit` the map is `factor*t + offset*factor`, and for `to_celsius` and `to_fahrenheit` it is `T/factor - offset`. The one function `affine_map_expression` defines their expansion: `Add(Mul(a, x), b)` with `a` and `b` as exact number leaves, so a plan has a `Mul` gate, then an `Add` gate, and never `MulAdd`. `to_coherent_units` checks the dimensions and then calls it:
- `from_*` needs a dimensionless reading and gives dimension Θ.
- `to_*` needs dimension Θ and gives a dimensionless reading.

Because `to_*` runs after the argument is converted to coherent units, `to_celsius(300000 mK)` is 26.85. A `degC` difference is accepted as a temperature. Exact evaluation, `evaluate_f64` lowering, the rounding bound enclosure and `with_propagated_uncertainty` all work on this one expansion, and the stored expression keeps its written shape. The rounding bound therefore counts the rounding of both constants and both gates. The phase 2 encloser of `evaluated_scores` works on the unexpanded derivation, and its derivative rule for these operators uses the same coefficients in the same order: the value is `a*x + b` and the gradient is scaled by `a`.

`search_ways` is the given-and-wanted search. It takes a `RuleSet` as plain data: quantities as role identifiers, and rules with an identifier, an output, inputs, the formula as an anonymous function in the pool, conditions, an exactness flag and sources. The request names the wanted quantity, the given quantities, the obtainable and not obtainable quantities, `only_obtainable`, the way cap and the work budget.

- Reachability runs the rules in generation rounds with a counter of missing premises per rule. It is exact and has no limit.
- The ways are the minimal input sets. The search expands backward over AND and OR in rounds of increasing depth, cuts repeated quantities on a path, reduces every candidate to a minimal set, and stops at the way cap or the work budget. The answer says whether and by what it was truncated, the last completed round, and how many ways were found. Every way with at most that many steps is listed.
- A not obtainable quantity is never an input. With `only_obtainable`, inputs come only from the marked quantities. A given quantity is known and never an input.
- Each way has its canonical derivation: the rounds from its inputs and the given, with the rule of the smallest identifier deriving each quantity. The way carries its steps, the rule identifiers of the derivation, its last rule, whether all inputs are given or marked, its exactness, error bound and gate count, and the conditions of its rules. The gate count lowers the derivation, substituted into one expression, to an `F64` plan.
- When no way exists under the marks, the answer is `NotReached` with the smallest further input sets over every quantity that is not marked not obtainable, one-element sets first. An empty list means no rule leads to the wanted quantity.

`list_forward` is the forward listing: the quantities reachable from the given roles, with the roles marked obtainable. It runs the rounds in two stages. Stage 1 starts from the given alone. Stage 2 continues from what stage 1 reached together with the obtainable roles, and its steps count on from the last round of stage 1. So a quantity that follows from the given is never derived through an obtainable role, not in its own entry and not inside a later one. Each entry carries its steps, the rule that yields it and that rule's inputs, the canonical derivation, the given or obtainable roles it uses, the conditions and sources of its whole derivation, and whether it needs an obtainable role. Given and obtainable roles are not listed. The entries are ordered by steps and then by name, only the list is capped, and the answer states the exact number of reachable quantities and whether the list was cut. An entry that follows from the given alone has the steps and derivation the way search gives it as the wanted quantity. `decide_derivation_conditions` decides the conditions of any derivation, such as an entry's, at given values.

`rank_ways` orders ways by a list of criteria combined by `order` (lexicographic) or `front` (Pareto layers), then by a fixed tie-break: exactness, error bound, gate count, last rule identifier, input set. Scores of smallest uncertainty and conditioning come from the caller as exact numbers and are unknown otherwise, so all ways tie on them. The order is total and depends only on the data.

`evaluated_scores` computes the phase 2 scores that `rank_ways` takes for a way, from given values in coherent units. It substitutes the way's canonical derivation into one expression over its leaf quantities with `derivation_expression`, and evaluates it once in interval arithmetic together with the interval gradient over the leaves (forward differentiation by the chain rule). Every given value enters as its enclosure.

- `smallest_uncertainty` is the upper end of the enclosure of the first-order propagated standard uncertainty `sqrt(Σ (∂f/∂x_i · u(x_i))²)` for independent inputs. An input without uncertainty contributes nothing, so a way whose inputs all are certain scores exactly 0.
- `conditioning` is the upper end of the enclosure of the componentwise relative condition number `Σ |∂f/∂x_i| · |x_i| / |f|`. For a sum it is the condition number of a finite sum. It is unknown where the value enclosure contains zero.
- Both upper ends are the exact rationals of `f64` ends rounded outward, so they are never smaller than the true value. A score is unknown when an operation has no enclosure or no derivative here, such as `abs` at zero.

`decide_conditions` decides every condition of the rules of a way's canonical derivation at given values, in derivation order, and returns `holds` per condition for the `conditions` of a way. A condition is instantiated over the expressions of its rule's inputs, so a condition on a derived quantity uses its derivation. Relations `==`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=` combine with `and`, `or` and `not` in three-valued logic. When every leaf value is exact, a relation is decided by the exact sign of the difference of its sides, which also covers square roots and multiples of pi. Otherwise, or when that sign is not available, such as for `sin`, it is decided by value enclosures, and it stays undecided when the enclosures overlap. A condition whose leaf values are missing is undecided (`null`).

`holds` has one three-valued definition for ways, forward entries and further inputs. A parameter whose role has a given value, or follows from given values along the derivation, takes its enclosure. Every other parameter is unknown, and a comparison with an unknown operand is undecided, while `and`, `or` and `not` combine by the strong three-valued rules. So a condition can be `false` before every value is known, when its known part already fails. `further_input_entries` gives each further input set of a not-reached answer its staged derivation, through `staged_derivation` in three stages (the given, then the obtainable roles, then the added roles), its last rule, and its conditions with `holds`. The bounds are never an input to `holds`. `not_reached_bounds` also lists in `from` each bound that gave a kept end, with its kind, sorted inputs and the ends it gave.

`not_reached_bounds` combines the bounds of the wanted role whose inputs all have values and whose conditions hold on the value enclosures. An `at-most` bound gives an inclusive upper end, `at-least` an inclusive lower end, and `strictly-between` two exclusive ends. An end is the exact rational of its formula when exact evaluation gives one, and otherwise the outward end of its enclosure. The tightest lower and upper ends are kept, an exclusive end wins over an inclusive one of the same value, and the sources of the bounds that gave the kept ends are listed. The ends and the conditions are computed at the stated values: a bound holds for those values and does not include their measurement uncertainty, which has no enclosure of its own. Every way lists the sources of the rules of its derivation, and `ComputedResult` carries sources.

`with_propagated_uncertainty` adds the measurement uncertainty to a result whose expression contains `Uncertain` (`2.5 +- 0.01`) or `UncertainExpanded` (`2.5 +- 0.02 (k=2)`) nodes. `central_expression` gives the same expression with every such node replaced by its value, so the caller evaluates the value exactly or in `F64` as for any line. Every distinct uncertain node is one input, with the standard uncertainty `u` or `U/k`. A node that occurs twice, such as a named result used twice, is one input, so `m*m` with `m = 2 +- 0.1` has the uncertainty 0.4. The combined standard uncertainty is the first-order estimate `sqrt(Σ (∂f/∂x_i · u_i)²)`, with the sensitivities computed in `F64` at the input values in coherent units by forward differentiation and the correctly rounded functions of `calc-numbers`. It is an estimate, stored as an `F64` value with coverage factor 1, and never the upper end of the `smallest_uncertainty` enclosure. The method keeps its name and gains the parameters `uncertainty_propagation` (`first_order_taylor`), `input_correlation` (`uncorrelated_distinct_inputs`), `assumes_linear_within_uncertainty` (`true`), `linearity_verified` (`false`) and `uncertain_input_count`, and the result cites P-GRD-D-038 and P-GRD-D-039. A negative uncertainty, a coverage factor below 1, an uncertainty of an uncertainty, a binder or operator without a derivative rule here (such as `factorial`), and a non-finite estimate are typed errors.

`evaluate_way` evaluates a way of a search into a `ComputedResult`. It substitutes the given values into the canonical derivation, a value with a standard uncertainty as an `Uncertain` node, evaluates the central value exactly when that gives a rational and otherwise in `F64` through the backends passed in, and adds the propagated uncertainty. The method is `way_evaluation` with the parameters `rule` and `concept_set_version`, and the record lists the sources of the way. Conditions of the way are not checked here.

`rational_form` decides the form of an exact rational result from its expression: `Decimal` when the expression contains a non-integer decimal literal or `exact` and every node is a terminating decimal literal, an integer, or one of the operations that keep a terminating decimal terminating, with the written-unit ratios of `Add`, `Sub`, `Min`, `Max`, the rounding operators, `ToCelsius`, `ToFahrenheit` and the root against the display unit free of primes other than 2 and 5 and of π; `Fraction` otherwise. A rational power or square root counts only when its exact result is rational.

`recognize` is the pattern matcher of concept recognition. The caller hands in every pattern as plain data (`RecognitionPattern`): concept and pattern identifier, a function whose parameters are the pattern variables, and its guard or way conditions as functions over the same parameters. calc-core does not know the learning graph.
- **Sites:** every distinct node of the expression, in pre-order, except one whose `Bound` indices escape it.
- **Matching:** Nodes match by identity, and constants match only the same interned node, so `3^2` never matches `9` and `f64'2'` never matches `2`. Whole `Add` and `Mul` chains, taken downward from a site, match in any pairing of operands with one operand per chain variable. Every other operator keeps its order. A repeated variable needs equivalent bindings under the same rule. Bindings never capture a binder outside the site. A pattern quantity matches the same unit only.
- **Conditions:** substituted and decided exactly with three values. Relations use the exact sign of the difference, which keeps exact evaluation's size limit. A false condition discards the match, and true or undecided keeps it with `holds` recorded. A machine operand is undecided.
- **Coverage and specificity:** coverage is the distinct nodes the match covers (the site, the chain nodes, the pattern's structure and everything inside the bindings) over the distinct nodes of the expression, as an exact rational. Specificity is the distinct non-variable nodes of the pattern body.
- **Offer threshold:** a match is offered at coverage of at least 1/2, specificity of at least 2 and no false condition. A match of specificity 1, one operator such as `a + b`, is offered only when it covers the whole expression and every condition is true, so `1 + 1` is recognized and the sum inside `sqrt(3^2 + 4^2)` is not. At the root, such a pattern of `+` or `·` that finds no chain of its length matches the written grouping, so `1+2+3` is `(1+2)+3`. Its conditions are decided at the root and at every node below it of the same operator, each bound to that node's written operands, so `0.5+0.5+2` is no sum of whole numbers.
- **Order:** every match of specificity 2 or more before every match of specificity 1, then coverage, specificity, all-true conditions before undecided ones, concept identifier, pattern identifier, site position, and the bindings printed by the caller's `print` function (calc-core has no printer). For one concept, pattern and site, only the first match is kept.
- **Duplicate check:** `equivalent_pattern_shapes` tells whether two patterns have the same shape under the matching rule with their variables renamed one to one, for the `calc-concepts` load check that rejects an explicit pattern repeating a derived one.
- **Limits:** `RecognitionLimits` has the cap (default 5, 1 to 100) and the work budget (default 100 000, 1 to 10 000 000), which counts chain pairings and condition decisions. The answer, `PatternRecognition`, lists the first matches up to the cap, with `truncated` and `truncated_by` (`Cap` or `WorkBudget`).

The working of a line. `exact_working` and `machine_working` give the steps that produced a value, recomputed from the expression and never stored. A step is one operation on its operands: its `path` from the root of the expression as operand indices, the operator or the reference it applies, each operand with its value and, where the operand is itself an operation, its own path, the result, and the kind, `Exact` or `Machine` with its domain. The steps come deepest first, so `(2 + 3) * 4` gives `2 + 3 = 5` and then `5 * 4 = 20`. `References` carries the value of each name that stands for another line, which becomes a step of its own, and the same values are substituted before a subexpression is evaluated, so a step's value is the value the line had. An exact step is evaluated by `evaluate_exact` and a machine step by `evaluate_f64` or `evaluate_f32` on the backends the caller passes, which are the ones the record names; a plan with an approximate operation is refused with `ApproximateOperations` instead of showing steps from an operation whose result may differ between backends. At most `STEP_LIMIT` steps, 200, are expanded per request, breadth first from the requested path; `further_steps` counts the steps left and `further_paths` names the subexpressions they sit in, each of which is a request of its own.

The named physical constants live here: `c_0`, `h_P`, `k_B`, `N_A` and `G_N`, each with the value and unit its source gives. `constant_expression` builds one as a quantity in its coherent units, and `G_N` is built with its standard uncertainty rather than as a bare number, so anything computed from it propagates that uncertainty while the four defining constants of the SI carry none. `physical_constant` looks one up by its spelling and `constant_of_bare_symbol` finds the constant a bare `c`, `h`, `k`, `N` or `G` carries, which is what the undefined-name message names. No spelling is reserved: a name a person defines wins, and a constant is used only where nothing else answers to that name.

A sum or a product over exact operands is evaluated exactly. `sum(i^2, i, 1, 10)` is the exact 385 and `product(k, k, 1, 200)` the exact 200 factorial, where both went to the f64 plan before and the product came back as `inf`. The bounds must be integer literals; each index is put in place of the bound variable by the same de Bruijn substitution the rule search uses, and the terms are added or multiplied exactly, so the binder form of the input makes no difference to the value and only the machine path needs it. The size limit is checked after every term, so a product that grows past it is refused with the typed size error rather than running on. An empty range gives the empty sum 0 and the empty product 1, whichever way round its bounds are. A range longer than 100 000 terms, a bound that is not an integer literal, and a body with a machine operand are left to the plan as before, and so is a reduction whose work runs past the budget: the cost of exact rational arithmetic is not linear in the number of terms, so every term whose running total is not a whole number is charged its bit length and a total above the budget hands the line to the plan. Whole numbers are not charged, so a long sum of integers and a large factorial stay exact while a harmonic sum stops after a thousand or so terms rather than running for hours. What keeps one term small is the size check and not the budget: the running total is held under the size limit of exact values after every term, so the budget buys at most a handful of terms at that size and the worst reduction costs the same order as a single exact operation on the largest exact value calc admits.

`chemistry` holds CIAAW's standard atomic weights of 2024 (`atomic_weights_2024.rs`, 84 elements, exact) and computes a molar mass as an exact range: each element's natural interval or value ± expanded uncertainty, summed and multiplied by the molar mass constant of CODATA 2022 carried as its value ± 2 standard uncertainties. `balance` balances a reaction on the exact kernel of the matrix of its conserved quantities, which the caller chooses (`chemical_quantities` gives the elements and, when charged, the charge). It scales a one-dimensional kernel to the smallest positive integers, names a species that takes no part or belongs on the other side, and for a larger kernel lists the smallest non-negative reactions and never one as the answer. Written coefficients are checked, a missing one counting as 1. `molar_mass(chem'…')` is a range node of the worst case, so it works inside arithmetic.

`nuclear` holds the measured atomic masses of AME2020 (`atomic_masses_2020.rs`, 2550 nuclides as printed in Part II Table I, exact, and the keys of the 1008 whose mass is only an estimate). `balance_nuclear` checks a nuclear reaction as written, with the mass number, the charge and the electron lepton number as the conserved quantities of `balance`, photons left out, and where the line fails names its first failing quantity and, when there is one, its only balance. `q_value` gives the Q value from atomic masses as an exact range in keV: each mass as its value ± 2 standard uncertainties and 3 units of its last printed digit (¹²C exact), u·c² and 2 m_e·c² of CODATA 2022 at 2 standard uncertainties, the ends added. `q_value(nuc'…')` is a range node of the worst case.

The masses in `atomic_masses_2020.rs` are carried unchanged, as printed, from M. Wang, W.J. Huang, F.G. Kondev, G. Audi and S. Naimi, "The AME 2020 atomic mass evaluation (II). Tables, graphs and references", Chinese Physics C 45 (2021) 030003, DOI 10.1088/1674-1137/abddaf, under the Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 licence (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). The method is in Part I, W.J. Huang et al., Chinese Physics C 45 (2021) 030002, DOI 10.1088/1674-1137/abddb0.

`reduced_quotient` reduces a line in free names to one quotient p / q. A sum is brought over the least common multiple of its denominators. p and q have no common factor, and are normalised jointly: whole coefficients with no common divisor and a positive leading coefficient of q, or q monic in its first term where a square root remains. Written denominators whose square-free part does not divide q are named in `excluding`. The gcd splits off common monomials, then uses Brown's dense modular algorithm for rational coefficients: images modulo primes below 2^63, Newton interpolation over the last name, Chinese remaindering, and trial division, which decides every result. For one name with square-root coefficients it uses the monic Euclidean algorithm over the field. In several names with square roots, `field_gcd` takes images modulo primes where every prime under a root is a square. There is one image for each choice of signs of those roots, and a Hadamard transform recovers the coefficient of each root. Images are combined by the Chinese remainder theorem and rational reconstruction, and the result is accepted by trial division over the field. `--identity` brings a claim over the least common multiple too.

`exact_complex` computes a line that holds `i` or `complex(a, b)` exactly, where it is built by +, −, ×, ÷ and whole powers from exact real values with square roots in their normal form.
- Division is by the conjugate over the norm, and a power is taken by repeated squaring, checked against the size limit of exact numbers at every step.
- A zero imaginary part gives a real value. Rational parts give a complex pair, and a square-root part gives an expression written with `* i`.
- `re`, `im`, `conj` and `abs` of such a value are exact too. A modulus outside the field is its exact root, as `sqrt(4 + 2 * sqrt(2))`, and an even power of a modulus is taken through the norm.
- For a real value, `re` and `conj` give the value and `im` gives 0.
- Anything else holding `i`, such as `pi*i` or `exp(i)`, is left to the paths that took it before.

`enclose_ode` encloses the solution of an initial value problem y' = f(t, y), y(t0) = y0, at requested times, by a Lohner-type interval Taylor method of order 20: a high-order existence test gives a rough enclosure per step, the Taylor coefficients come by interval automatic differentiation of f, and the solution set is carried in a QR-chosen basis. Time and the initial values are exact numbers. It reports the enclosure at each time, the number of steps, and, per component, the largest per-step remainder bound, which is the truncation error of one step and not a bound on the trajectory. Initial values are intervals, so a value known only as an enclosure (π, √2) is carried as one; the refusal at the step limit gives the last step length. The step length is estimated from ratios of Taylor coefficients, and a step is shortened until its remainder is below the unit roundoff of f64 times the size the solution reaches over the step, so the relative width of an answer does not depend on the unit it is written in. An operation without a series here, a division by a constant that exact evaluation decides is zero, a machine-number time, a stop before the start, a step that cannot be taken after 40 halvings (a solution that blows up) and more than 10 000 steps are typed refusals.

## How to test

`cargo test -p calc-core`

The square root arithmetic is checked with the trigonometric Pythagoras and the addition theorem over all table angles, and signs are compared with floating-point values far from zero. Machine evaluation has a test per operator that runs the plan on `calc-exec-cpu` and checks the value and that the bound covers the distance to the exact value or to a 30-digit reference, while staying below 10^-12. Mutations of the sign rule, the conjugate, the prime products, the table, the rounding tie rule, the quadrant rule, the exponent sign and the root check each make a test fail.
