# calc-render

## What it does

The CPU reference rasterizer of the `Scene` of `calc-viz`, the text path with the bundled fonts, and image encoding. It performs no input or output: it returns pixels and encoded bytes, and the frontends write files and windows.

Shared with the cockpit:

- `canvas` rasterizes a draw list with tiny-skia. Rectangles and frames are filled on whole pixels without antialiasing. Rounded rectangles use quadratic corners with antialiasing. For pictures it also fills and strokes paths inside a clip mask, dashed where asked, and sets pixels directly.
- `colour` and `theme` hold the light and dark theme tokens, including one colour per result kind.
- `draw` is the list of drawing commands a canvas executes.
- `geometry` converts logical sizes to physical pixels and snaps line widths, and holds the cell size of the text grid.
- `digits` rounds decimal value texts without floating point.
- `text::font` loads Noto Sans Mono and Noto Sans Math from `fonts/`, `text::shape` shapes with harfrust, `text::grid` derives the cell and baseline, `text::cells` counts cells, `text::placement` places clusters on the cell grid, `text::render` draws glyph outlines from skrifa, and `text::line` draws data text at a grid origin.

Added for pictures:

- `image` holds an `Image` of straight RGBA bytes and encodes it as PAM (P7, RGB_ALPHA) and as PNG with one IDAT, Adler-32 and CRC-32. A PNG whose data stream would not fit one chunk is a typed error.
- The PNG is compressed: every row is coded with the PNG filter, of the five, whose coded bytes read as signed values have the smallest sum, and `deflate` writes one final block with the fixed Huffman codes of RFC 1951 over the filtered rows, matching in a 32 768 byte window through a hash of three bytes and a chain of at most 128 earlier positions. It is in-house, needs no crate and is deterministic, so golden images stay byte comparisons; `calc plot`'s 800 by 600 picture is about 31 kB where uncompressed stored blocks gave 1.9 MB. The test module reads every stream back with a fixed Huffman inflater, and the decision oracle has its own, written from the format rather than from this encoder.
- The match chain is indexed by `position & (WINDOW - 1)`, so a slot can hold the predecessor of a position 32 768 earlier, and a walk can meet a candidate that belongs to another position. That costs ratio and never correctness: every value written into the chain is smaller than the position it was written for, so the distance cannot underflow; a candidate is only used after `match_length` has compared the real bytes; and the walk is bounded by the chain limit, so an aliased chain that stops decreasing cannot loop.
- `plot_layout` returns the plot area of each view from the image size, the text size, the scale factor and the view kinds alone. A plane view reserves a gutter for the widest tick label the format allows, `TICK_LABEL_CELLS`, a tick label row and an axis title row; every picture reserves a key row and a footer row. `ViewLayout::divisions` gives the divisions of a plot request: the pixel counts of a plane view, and the smaller of the two on every axis of a space view.
- `tick_label` writes a tick value with digits, the caller's decimal separator, a minus sign and a power-of-ten exponent, and nothing else. Linear ticks are exact multiples of 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, spaced by their widest label; logarithmic ticks are powers of ten. A tick whose label would be wider than the format allows is not drawn, so a deep zoom shows only the round ticks whose labels fit.
- `render_scene` checks the scene and draws one frame. Views lie side by side in equal columns. Coordinates are mapped after subtracting the view origin in the column's own domain. A plane view draws polylines broken at non-finite values, runs of unresolved samples as the band of their enclosure, a curve with column enclosures column by column: as the line where the column is resolved to a value step, with its proven span solid where that reaches more than a line width beyond the line, as its enclosure in the curve colour at reduced opacity with its proven span solid where it is not, and hatched where it is marked, bands, points with markers, arrows, scalar and complex grids cell by cell in their colour maps with missing, unresolved, inside and undecided cells in their roles, triangle meshes, graphs, formula text and bit layouts, then the frame, ticks and axis titles with each axis symbol. A view that holds only figures has no frame, ticks or titles. A space view shows the visible box as a cube from the camera angles with the reference sine and cosine and draws meshes, voxels and volume cells through a depth buffer, with back faces in their own colour. Figures are drawn with 1 px lines and 2 px outlines, arcs of 2 em with further arcs 0.3 em apart, the square and the dot, ticks, labels outside their element or inside the arc, and the key; the placed layout is returned as `FigureLayout` for `check_label_layout`. The key row names every mark kind and the escape-time classes, and the footer row shows the sketch word, the precision notices and the undecided share, all from caller text in `PictureText`. Text that does not fit its row is a typed error, never a smaller plot.
- `legend` draws the colour-map legend rows between the key row and the footer, one per colour-mapped layer in view and layer order: the caller's legend title, then 16 swatches for a sequential map, 17 for a diverging one, or 12 hue and 8 lightness swatches for domain colouring, each 1 em wide and 0.8 em high above a text row whose labels stand at their own positions. A range end or centre is labelled exactly when the tick format can write it, and otherwise by the nearest round tick inside the range. An escape-time legend adds the Inside and Undecided swatches with their class names, which wrap onto reserved rows when they do not fit; those classes leave the key row. `plot_layout` takes the legend requests, which frontends build from `shape_legend` before sampling, so the plot area stays known before sampling.
- A space view cuts a surface at the faces of its visible box, so its cut edge shows. A legend end label reads `≤` or `≥` before its number when a finite value of the layer lies beyond that end of the colour range. When the tick format cannot write that end, the number is the end rounded outward to two significant digits and the round tick standing in for it is not drawn; every marked end label stands flush with the outer edge of its bar, and a diverging legend puts its centre label on a second label row that it always reserves.
- `undecided_share` computes the footer share of undecided cells from the diagnostics.
- `Scene`, `Interval` and `Number` are re-exported so that the frontends, which have no edge to `calc-viz` or `calc-numbers`, can hold scenes and build view states.

Picture roles are shapes in the text tones of the theme, anchored to the plot area: a hatch for missing values, the opposite hatch for marked columns and a stipple for unresolved samples and bands in the secondary tone, the curve colour at reduced opacity for what may be hit, a cross-hatch for undecided cells in the tertiary tone, solid text for inside cells with a window-coloured line just inside every inside region, solid tertiary tone for back faces, and provisional layers blended halfway to the window. Every role a picture shows gets a swatch and a word in the key row. Figure segments are stroked on the device pixel grid.

A draw list can frame a rounded rectangle as well as fill one, which is what a reading ring is drawn with. It also fills a quadrilateral as one path, so a square turned or upright has no seam along a diagonal, and fills or frames an ellipse, drawn as an oval path and not as a rounded rectangle, whose quadratic corners bulge and cannot make a circle; the frame stays inside its rectangle, as a framed rectangle's does. These draw the shapes of the figure elements. The tick gutter of a plane view is measured from the settled range each axis carries in the layout request: `LayoutRequest.views` holds a `ViewRequest` per view with an `AxisRange` per axis, `Some` where a settled range is known and `None` where it is not, and the gutter is as wide as the widest label the axis's ticks produce in the plot area it would have under the default eleven-cell gutter, measured once and never as a fixed point. An axis with no settled range keeps those eleven cells, which is where a value axis stands until `calc-viz` has settled its range. `RenderRequest.settled` overrides the scene's own ranges, so a caller that must hold one gutter across scenes or through a gesture passes the settled views it laid out with; without it the scene's ranges are used, which is right for a picture laid out from the range it was sampled at.

A `DrawnScene` also reports `axis_units`, the rectangle of the unit part of each view's axis title, one `Option<PhysicalRect>` per axis in axis order, `Some` where the title ends in a unit symbol and `None` where it does not. It is filled by the drawing that places the text, from the same left edge and the same widths, so a frontend can underline or hit the unit without composing the title a second time. A space view reports one per axis of its joined title row. The measurement holds because a cell width is the same for every character of the monospace grid, so the width of a title is the sum of the widths of its parts wherever it is split at a character boundary; a proportional font would end that and the tail would have to be measured with the head. A view that draws no axes, such as a figure, reports an empty list rather than a list of `None`, so a reader takes an axis's entry with `get` and not by indexing.

`axis_unit_rects` reports the same rectangles without drawing anything, from a `PictureLayout`, the scene, the frame index and the picture's words. A frontend that draws a picture behind a shared reference cannot keep what the draw returned, and a pointer press arrives a frame later, so the rectangles have to be reachable from the state it does keep, which is the layout. The two cannot drift, because both go through one placement: `axis_units` holds where a title is put in its row and where its trailing symbol falls, the drawing draws at the left that placement gives it, and the measurement reads the rectangle from the same call. The layout pass itself cannot carry the rectangles, because it runs before sampling and a title's width depends on the unit symbol the scene resolves.

`draw_scene` draws a scene into a canvas the caller owns, at an offset, and gives back the layout it used, moved to that offset; the picture's own rectangle is filled with the window colour first and nothing outside it is touched, so a frontend can put a picture inside a frame it is already drawing without an image in between. `render_scene` is that call on a canvas of the picture's size, and returns the image, which the CLI writes as a PNG.

## How to test

`cargo test -p calc-render`

Unit tests draw scenes built from `calc-viz` types and check exact pixels, for every primitive kind and every `RenderError` variant, and check layouts, ticks, mapping, clipping, the depth buffer and both encoders. The font tests check that every font file has a sources row with licence OFL-1.1.

`tests/golden_images.rs` renders seven scenes from `tests/golden/scenes.rs`, a curve with a gap and an enclosure, an escape-time grid with its classes, a right triangle, a ridge surface in a space view whose front slope hides part of the back slope and whose far side shows back faces, a diverging grid with a missing cell, a domain-coloured grid, and a plane mesh with a missing vertex, at 640 by 480 px and compares them byte for byte with the PAM files beside them, on x86_64 only. The bytes depend on the target features of the build.

The golden images are regenerated only by running `cargo run -p calc-render --example bless`, which renders the scenes of `tests/golden/scenes.rs` and overwrites the PAM files; no test or build step writes them. A golden changes only in a commit that says so, and a change to golden files is reviewed by looking at the regenerated images.

`crates/calc-cli/tests/plot_picture.rs` runs a line through the plot job of `calc-app` into `render_scene`.
